## Context

An R Shiny dashboard that segments credit card customers by how they spend. One
`app.R` of about 730 lines, five tabs, over a 50,000-transaction file with customer,
merchant, category, amount and date columns. Built March 2025.

Its job on this site is narrow and I'd rather say so: it's the R and second
visualisation stack, next to a portfolio that is otherwise Python and TypeScript.

## The problem

A rewards programme costs money per customer and pays back unevenly. The question the
dashboard exists to answer is which cohorts are worth paying for: who spends enough to matter, in which
categories, and when.

## Constraints

**"Segmentation" here is cohorting, not clustering.** There's no k-means, no
hierarchical clustering, no PCA anywhere in the app. I checked, and it's worth being straight about because the project title implies a model. Customers are grouped by
rules I chose: spending tier, age band, gender, category. That's a defensible choice
for a dashboard someone has to read and act on, and it is not an unsupervised method.

**Everything recomputes on every filter change.** Five interacting filters over 50,000
rows, with each tab's plots derived from the filtered frame. Fine at this size, and the thing that would
need rethinking first at ten times the data.

## What I built

Five tabs, each answering a different question. Overview gives category dominance and
the demographic split. Demographics breaks spend into histograms and tiers with
transaction size and frequency. Trends covers monthly spending with peaks marked and
day-of-week patterns. Key Customers and Merchants ranks both, including the top
merchants for high spenders in Travel specifically. Recommendations states the
actions the rest of the dashboard supports.

Built with shinydashboard and shinydashboardPlus, dplyr for the reshaping, ggplot2 and
plotly for the charts, DT for the tables.

## Limitations

The dataset's provenance isn't recorded, which matters more than it would on a
technical project: the conclusions are about customer behaviour, and I can't say whose.
The cohorts are rules rather than discovered structure, so they'd need revisiting for
a different customer base rather than re-fitting. And there's no test of whether the tiers are the right cut points. They're a judgement, applied
consistently.
